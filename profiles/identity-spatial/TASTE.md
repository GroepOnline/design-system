# Taste profile: identity-spatial

Use this profile for identity, access, invitation, consent and trust-entry surfaces where the product should feel like entering a place rather than filling in a generic form.

## Prefer

- One domain-native spatial metaphor that explains the product. Entry, boundary, permission, hand-off or identity may become a portal, threshold, frame, seal or other specific object.
- One dominant object per viewport, supported by typography and negative space rather than a collection of decorative cards.
- Asymmetric composition with a clear reading path. The visual anchor may occupy one side while the decision surface owns the other.
- Material cues that feel deliberate and tactile but stay subordinate to the actual decision.
- A display/body type contrast strong enough to create authorship without damaging form clarity.
- Motion that explains arrival, layer separation or state change. Keep it finite and stop it under reduced motion.
- A clear register shift after entry: expressive arrival surfaces, calmer editorial/product surfaces for account management.
- Real states and permissions. The visual language must never imply access, health or verification that the server did not establish.

## Reject

- Centered OAuth card as the whole composition when the product brief allows a full-page identity experience.
- Generic dark mesh, purple/blue AI gradients, glowing blobs, glass everywhere, bento-card farms or three-card feature strips.
- Decorative security theatre: fake locks, shields, green checkmarks or trust badges without real meaning.
- Multiple competing hero illustrations or signature interactions.
- Infinite ambient motion around a security decision.
- Reusing the same portal/object on unrelated products just because it looked good once.

## Gate

Before implementation, the agent must be able to answer in one sentence: “What does the signature object mean in this product?” If the answer is only “it looks premium”, the direction is not specific enough.
